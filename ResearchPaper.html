Deterrence stability and hypersonic delivery systems in South Asia

Abstract

The introduction of hypersonic glide vehicles and manoeuvrable re-entry systems compresses the time available to decision-makers in a crisis. This paper examines how compressed warning times interact with existing command and control arrangements between India and Pakistan. Using three crisis case studies and a structured review of declared doctrine, it argues that the main risk is not first-strike incentive but misreading: a launch that cannot be characterised in time is treated as the worst case.

DeterrenceHypersonic weaponsCrisis stabilityCommand and controlSouth Asia

1. Introduction

Strategic stability in South Asia has rested on an assumption that both sides would have time to understand what was happening before they had to act. That assumption is now under pressure.

Hypersonic systems do not change the arithmetic of deterrence. They change its tempo.

2. Method

The study combines a structured review of declared doctrine with three crisis case studies: 2001 to 2002, 2016 and 2019. Each case is coded for warning time, decision layers and the channels used to communicate intent.
