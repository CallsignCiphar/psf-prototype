Deterrence stability and hypersonic delivery systems in South Asia

Abstract

The introduction of hypersonic glide vehicles compresses the time available to decision-makers in a crisis. This paper examines how compressed warning times interact with command and control arrangements...

1. Introduction

Strategic stability in South Asia has rested on an assumption that both sides would have time to understand what was happening before they had to act. That assumption is now under pressure.

Hypersonic systems do not change the arithmetic of deterrence. They change its tempo. This paper asks what happens to crisis decision-making when the window between detection and impact falls below the time needed to characterise a launch.

2. Method

The study combines a structured review of declared doctrine with three crisis case studies. Each case is coded for warning time, decision layers and the channels used to communicate intent.

The coding framework follows [Author, year], adapted for a dyadic context.
